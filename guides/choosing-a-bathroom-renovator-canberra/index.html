How to choose a bathroom renovator in Canberra

Before you hire anyone to renovate your bathroom in Canberra, check the licence, the insurance, the warranty and the quote. This guide shows you how, and what to ask.

Written by Oleg, Owner of Apex Bathrooms ACT, renovating Canberra bathrooms since 2008. Updated 20 September 2026

Three quotes tell you nothing until you can see what each one has left out. Most of what separates a good renovator from a bad one is written down somewhere, and you can check all of it in an afternoon.

Do you need a renovator at all?

Doing it yourself saves the labour, which is usually the largest line in the quote, and it lets you shop your own fixtures and choose every finish. What it costs you is time: weeks or months of it, around work and family. It also carries the risk of work you have not done before, which often ends with a professional called in at a higher price than hiring one at the start. Electrical, plumbing, cabinetry and waterproofing go to a licensed trade in every case. The membrane needs a licensed waterproofer's certificate at the end of it, and the rest of that decision is set out in our guide to bathroom renovation mistakes and when DIY makes sense.

Licences: the ACT and NSW are different

This is the one people get wrong. Building and construction licensing in the ACT is administered by Access Canberra. New South Wales has its own system under NSW Fair Trading. They are separate jurisdictions with separate registers.

So ask two questions. Which licence does this business hold, and what is the number? Then look the number up on the register for the jurisdiction your house sits in. A tradesperson working on both sides of the border needs to be licensed for both sides.

A government logo on a website is not a licence. Neither is the word "licensed" in a heading. Association membership is checkable too - ask for the membership number and look it up rather than trusting the badge.

Ask us the same question. We will give you the licence number and the jurisdiction it is held in verify. Look it up on the register before you call us back. That is exactly what we are telling you to do with everyone else you ask.

What insurance actually covers

"Fully insured" is not a statement about anything. Ask for the certificate of currency, check the date on it, and check that the cover matches the value of the work.

Two different things are worth asking about. Public liability covers damage and injury on your property while the work is going on. Separately, residential building work in the ACT can require statutory insurance above a certain value of work verify, so ask whether your job falls over that line and who is carrying it.

Ask us for ours and we will send you the certificate of currency, with the insurer named and the date on it, rather than the word "insured" on a web page verify.

The warranty question, asked properly

"A 10-year warranty" and "up to a 10-year warranty" are different promises, and plenty of renovation websites use both on the same page. Ask four things:

  • What exactly does it cover - workmanship, the membrane, the fixtures?
  • For how long, and from what date?
  • What is excluded?
  • Does it transfer if you sell the house?

Then ask for it in writing. A warranty you cannot produce on paper is a sentence on a website. Our installation warranty is written out in full verify and you get the documentation at handover, along with the product warranties for every fixture.

Reading a quote: what must be itemised

A quote you can compare lists:

  • demolition and waste removal, separately, not bundled into one line;
  • waterproofing, with the membrane type, the coverage area, and the compliance certificate included;
  • tiling, with labour and materials split, and a tile allowance per square metre;
  • plumbing, with fixture connections and any relocation clearly noted;
  • the electrical scope, itemised;
  • fixtures and fittings, either supplied by the contractor or shown as Prime Cost allowances;
  • the project management or builder's margin, stated openly;
  • how variations and unexpected costs will be handled.

It should also come with a written project timeline: start date, milestones, estimated completion. What is included in a bathroom renovation goes through each of those line items in detail, and the cost guide has the numbers to check them against.

All residential building work should be backed by a written contract verify. If the job is being done on an emailed paragraph and a handshake, you have no mechanism when something goes wrong.

Six red flags

  1. No mention of a waterproofing certificate. Walk away. It is a legal requirement under the National Construction Code, not an optional extra.
  2. No breakdown between labour and materials. Without it you cannot compare two quotes at all.
  3. An allowance that is obviously too low. A tile or fixture allowance that could not buy decent materials for a room that size is a variation waiting to happen.
  4. No site inspection. Quoting without seeing the bathroom produces ballpark estimates, not binding prices.
  5. A price well below the rest. A quote well below the ranges in our cost guide usually means something is excluded.
  6. A promise to demolish, waterproof and tile in three or four days. The membrane needs a minimum of 24 hours between coats and another 24 to 48 hours before tiling. Cutting that is one of the most common causes of waterproofing failure, and it is invisible once the tiles are laid. Our guide to bathroom renovation mistakes covers the rest of them.

One more test, which never appears on the quote at all: a renovator who does not inspect the substrate before quoting, and does not tell you up front what it might cost if there is damage underneath. Older homes here - ex-government houses and 1960s to 1980s brick homes in Belconnen, Tuggeranong, Woden and the Inner North - routinely hide compressed fibre cement sheeting and subfloors that have been wet for decades.

The questions to ask before you sign

  • Are you licensed, and can I see your licence number?
  • Who performs the waterproofing - you, or a subcontractor?
  • Will you issue a waterproofing compliance certificate?
  • Is your quote fixed-price, or subject to variations?
  • What happens if you find hidden damage under the tiles?
  • What does the warranty cover, for how long, and is it in writing?
  • Who will actually be on site, and who manages the job day to day?
  • Can I speak to two recent customers, or see an address where you have worked?

That last one matters more than testimonials. A quote on a website cannot be checked; a review on a Google Business Profile can, and a customer you can ring can tell you what the job was like in week three. So ask us for it: two recent customers you can ring. Our reviews name the suburb each job was in verify, and the projects, named by suburb and room are there to be checked the same way.

Queanbeyan: the border matters

Renovation businesses here advertise "Canberra and Queanbeyan" constantly and almost none of them mention that Queanbeyan is in New South Wales. It is a short drive from Fyshwick, so plenty of trades work both sides, but the paperwork is different.

For a Queanbeyan job, licensing, approvals and consumer protection follow NSW rules. Approvals go through a council verify, which is not how it works in the ACT - there are no local councils in the ACT, and a development approval comes from the ACT Government's planning authority verify. If a website tells you to "check with your local council" about a Canberra bathroom, it was not written for you.

When to start looking

Good renovators are booked ahead, so start the conversation before you are ready to start the job. A business with no waiting list at all is worth a second question. Use that lead time properly: set the budget first and let it dictate the scope, decide the style, research fixtures and materials for durability as well as looks, and get quotes from several licensed tradespeople.

What should a bathroom renovation quote itemise? Demolition and waste separately, waterproofing with the membrane and certificate, tiling with labour and materials split, plumbing, electrical, fixtures or allowances, the margin, and how variations are handled.

One quote is well below the others. Why? Usually because something has been left out, or will come back later as a variation verify. Compare the inclusions line by line before you compare the totals.
