A full bathroom renovation

As a bathroom ages it starts to cost you money. Outdated plumbing leads to leaks and, eventually, to structural repairs. Old fixtures push up water use and your bills. Worn surfaces hold mould and mildew, which is a health problem as well as a looks problem. And an older bathroom has none of the safety features a current one does.

A full renovation deals with all of it at once. We strip the room back, prepare and moisture-test the substrate, waterproof it, tile it, and fit the plumbing, electrical and fixtures. We can recreate an existing ensuite, or build and retile a bathroom, powder room or separate toilet from scratch. If you want the scope set out line by line before you talk to anyone, read what a renovation includes and what it does not.

Custom design

A custom bathroom is designed around how you use the room rather than around what already fits. We start with the shape of the space, because that decides where the vanity, showerhead, toilet and bath can sensibly go, and we make sure everything fits together with enough room to move.

From there it is material choices - tile, stone, timber and glass each cost differently, wear differently and give the room a different feel - and the extras worth building in while the walls are open: a shower niche, a heated towel rail, better lighting, better ventilation. Modern, classic or vintage, from a small powder room to a large ensuite. There is more on styles and layouts in our guide to choosing a bathroom design.

Small bathrooms and ensuites

Plenty of homes here were built between the 1960s and the 1980s, and their bathrooms are small, original and laid out for a different way of living. Small is a design problem, not a defect.

The moves that work are unglamorous and reliable. A wall-hung toilet or a floating vanity leaves floor visible underneath, which makes the room read larger. A big mirror reflects light and adds apparent depth. Wall-mounted cabinets give you storage without eating the floor. A combination bath-shower, or shelving built into the wall, saves the space a freestanding unit would take. Our guide to small bathrooms and ensuites goes through the rest.

Luxury bathrooms

At the top end the work is the same and the specification is not: a freestanding bath, a large-format porcelain or natural stone finish, heated floors, considered lighting, a properly sized extraction fan. Done well it is the room that changes how the whole house feels, and it adds real value. We will tell you honestly which upgrades change the room and which only change the invoice.

Commercial bathrooms

If you run a business in Canberra, your bathroom is one of the things a customer quietly judges you on. We fit out commercial bathrooms of most types and sizes for business owners, property managers and builders: design consultation, tile installation, cabinetry and fixture upgrades, and the plumbing behind them.

The looks divide roughly three ways - traditional in marble, granite and ceramic; contemporary in stainless steel, glass and natural stone; modern in bold colour, concrete, metal and timber - and the specification is driven by traffic. In a busy amenity, porcelain outlasts almost everything else.

How the job runs

First a free measure and quote. We look at the bathroom you have, listen to what you want from it, inspect the substrate condition where we can, and give you a written, itemised quote - no ballpark figures.

Then design and planning, once you are happy with the quote: fixtures, tiles, finishes, and the order the trades come in.

Then the work. Demolition and substrate preparation, waterproofing to AS3740:2021 with the cure times the standard requires, tiling, then fixtures, fit-out and a thorough clean-up. A standard bathroom runs two to four weeks; four to six if plumbing moves or joinery is made to order. Our guide to how a renovation runs, week by week sets out each stage and what can stretch it.

You get a handover pack at the end: the waterproofing compliance certificate, the installation warranty paperwork, product warranties for the fixtures, and care instructions for the tiles, grout and silicone.

Frequently asked questions

Do you renovate ensuites and small bathrooms?
Yes. Ensuites and small original bathrooms are most of what we are asked for here, and small is a design problem rather than a defect. Our guide to small bathrooms and ensuites covers what makes one feel bigger without moving a wall.
Do I need approval to renovate a bathroom in the ACT?
Most standard renovations need no development approval. Structural changes - a load-bearing wall, the roofline, the footprint of the house - may need a development approval from the ACT Government's planning authority - there are no local councils in the ACT - and heritage-listed properties can need more verify. All the work must meet the National Construction Code and AS3740:2021.
Can I stay in the house while you work?
Yes, if you have a second bathroom. With only one, plan to make other arrangements for about two weeks, from demolition until the new fixtures work. We set the schedule to suit you and keep the site clean.
Do you handle the plumbing and electrical, or do I book those?
We handle them. Plumbing, electrical, waterproofing, tiling and the fit-off sit in one itemised quote and one program, and Oleg schedules the trades, so you are not chasing anyone. A licensed waterproofer inspects the membrane and issues the compliance certificate before any tiling starts.
What warranty comes with the work?
Apex Bathrooms ACT offers a 10-year installation warranty verify. What it covers and for how long is confirmed in writing in your quote, before any work starts.
