The job, photographed

The brief

The ensuite was original to the house: a shower over a hob that leaked at one corner, a vanity with no storage, and an exhaust fan that never cleared the steam. It is the ensuite an ex-government house was built with, and it came with the set of complaints those rooms always come with. The owners wanted the shower squared up and somewhere to put things, and they wanted to stop repainting the ceiling.

What we did

The room was stripped back to the frame and rebuilt, with the waterproofing inspected and certified before any tile went down. Demolition and substrate prep, with the substrate inspected and moisture tested. Plumbing adjusted for the new fixture positions. Waterproofing, inspected and certified by a licensed waterproofer. Wall and floor tiling. Cabinetry and benchtop install, then fit-off and clean-up.

The substrate under the old shower had been wet long enough to need replacing, which was found on day one, priced that day and repaired before the membrane went on.

Products and finishes

  • Wall-hung vanity with a solid-surface top
  • Frameless shower screen, measured after tiling
  • 600x1200mm porcelain wall tile, floor to ceiling
  • Small-format porcelain on the shower floor, following the fall to the drain
  • Matte black mixer, rail shower and towel rail
  • Ducted exhaust fan sized to the room

How long it took

Three weeks. The joinery lead time, not the tiling, set the finish date.
